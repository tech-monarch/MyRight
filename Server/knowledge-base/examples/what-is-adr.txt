What is Alternative Dispute Resolution?

Alternative Dispute Resolution, usually shortened to ADR, describes ways
of resolving a disagreement between two or more people or organizations
without going through a full court trial. Common forms of ADR include
negotiation, mediation, and arbitration.

Negotiation is the most informal option. The people involved discuss the
disagreement directly, sometimes with the help of a legal representative,
and try to reach an agreement between themselves.

Mediation involves a neutral third party, a mediator, who helps both
sides communicate and explore possible resolutions. A mediator does not
decide the outcome, the two sides reach their own agreement with the
mediator's guidance. Mediation is generally faster and less expensive
than going to court, and gives both sides more control over the outcome
than a court judgment would.

Arbitration is more formal than mediation. A neutral arbitrator hears
both sides and then makes a binding decision, similar to a judge, but
outside the court system.

General considerations when choosing between these options include: how
urgent the matter is, whether an ongoing relationship needs to be
preserved (mediation tends to be better for this than litigation), the
amount in dispute relative to the cost of each process, and whether
either side needs a legally binding, enforceable decision (which points
toward arbitration or litigation rather than negotiation or mediation).
