What generally happens during mediation

Mediation processes vary between institutions, but a typical process
looks roughly like this:

1. Intake: one party requests mediation and describes the dispute.
2. Invitation: the other party is invited to take part. Mediation is
   normally voluntary, both sides generally need to agree to participate.
3. Mediator assignment: a neutral mediator is assigned to the case.
4. Preparation: each side may be asked to share relevant documents or a
   short written summary of their position before the session.
5. Joint session: both parties meet with the mediator, either together
   or sometimes separately, to discuss the dispute.
6. Negotiation: the mediator helps the parties explore possible
   resolutions and work toward common ground.
7. Outcome: if the parties reach an agreement, it is usually written down
   and signed by both sides. If they do not reach an agreement, they
   remain free to pursue other options, including court.

Typical documents that help a mediation process move faster include any
written agreement or contract between the parties, receipts or proof of
payment, dated photographs, and copies of written communication (emails,
text messages) between the parties about the dispute.
